Your device identity

An ECDSA P-256 key is created on this device. It is stored as non-extractable, so page scripts can use it but can never read or export it.

Pair with someone (do this once, in person or over a channel you already trust)

  1. A taps Create pairing code and gives it to B (by QR photo, AirDrop or Signal, or by reading it out in person).
  2. B pastes it under Import, then sends back the reply code that appears.
  3. A imports B's reply. Now both devices share a secret and each knows the other's public key.

    Honest threat model

    Handshake does not detect deepfakes. It does not analyse audio or video at all. It answers one narrower question: is the other side holding the key you exchanged with this person earlier?

    It helps against

    • A cloned voice or face of your CFO or colleague calling from a new account or number. The impostor doesn't have the device key, so they can't produce the code or answer a challenge.
    • Forged approvals in chat: a token from an unpaired key, or one that has been edited or has expired, fails.
    • Simple replay: codes are tied to one direction and expire after about 30 s; challenges are random.

    It does NOT help against

    • A compromised or stolen unlocked device, malware, or a real person who is coerced or colluding. Handshake proves who holds the key, not who that human is or what they intend.
    • Live relay: an attacker who can talk to the real person at the same moment could ask them for a fresh code and pass it on. Use challenge-response and say why you're asking. Codes are short (6 digits, about 20 bits), so treat them as one signal among several.
    • A bad pairing: if the first exchange was intercepted, everything after it is meaningless. Pair in person or over a channel you already trust.
    • Secrets live in this browser's storage. If you clear site data, they're gone, and there's no recovery or sync.
    • Passkey checks show that a user-verifying authenticator approved the token. Without attestation, they don't prove which hardware it was.

    Privacy

    No server, no accounts, no analytics, no biometrics leave your device. Tokens include your chosen display name, the public key, the statement and timestamps, and nothing else.
